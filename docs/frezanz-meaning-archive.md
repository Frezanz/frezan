## Rules of the archive

1. Only meanings personally proposed by Frezanz and explicitly confirmed by Frezanz count as confirmed entries.
2. Ideas under discussion may be recorded separately as *candidates*, but they do not count toward the 1,000.
3. Each batch contains up to 10 proposed meanings. After each batch, Frezanz reviews and confirms which entries should be added.
4. Preserve the original wording and intent. Do not silently rewrite a proposed meaning into a different idea.
5. **Before adding any meaning, compare it against every existing candidate and confirmed meaning.** Check the full archive, not just the latest batch.
6. Count each distinct meaning only once. Rewording an existing meaning does not create a new entry.
7. Similar meanings may coexist only when they express a genuinely different nuance, scope, or idea. Explain the distinction before treating them as unique.
8. Keep a stable number for every confirmed unique meaning (001–1000).

## Duplicate-check procedure

Run this check for **every proposed meaning**, including meanings proposed one at a time and meanings inside a batch:

1. Compare its core idea, not just its wording, against all confirmed entries and all pending candidates.
2. Classify the result:
   - **Unique:** no existing entry expresses the same meaning.
   - **Exact duplicate:** same meaning and substantially the same wording.
   - **Semantic duplicate:** different wording, same underlying meaning.
   - **Related / overlapping:** shares an idea but may contain a distinct nuance or scope.
3. If it is an exact or semantic duplicate, do not add it as a new numbered meaning. Point to the existing entry and explain the match.
4. If it is related or overlapping, explain both meanings and the possible distinction. Ask Frezanz whether the distinction is intentional before counting it separately.
5. Keep alternate phrasings as aliases or notes under the original entry when useful; do not inflate the count.
6. Record the duplicate-check result in the review log so repeated ideas remain traceable.

**The target is 1,000 unique meanings, not 1,000 differently worded sentences.**

### Candidate A — Subjective-truth acceptance

> Acceptance of something as true from another person's perspective, without requiring that it be objectively or universally true.

### Candidate B — Perspective recognition

> Accepting another person's perspective or interpretation as theirs, without automatically agreeing with it or imposing one's own interpretation.

### Candidate C — Personal meaning recognition

> Recognizing that a person can give something a meaning that is valid within their own experience, even when that meaning is not universal.

**Initial duplicate-review note:** Candidates A, B, and C are related and may overlap. These distinctions are provisional, not confirmed. They must be reviewed together before any of them are counted as unique confirmed entries.
